## Answers captured during play

- 2026-07-31, engine-direction interview (owner chat):
  - Look target: clean/readable blended with photoreal AAA. High fidelity that stays legible; neither alone.
  - Combat feel: blend of positional outplay, weight/impact, and build-decided. No single north star; cinematic mocap-tier is not the priority by itself.
  - Platforms: desktop first. Wants mobile optionality kept open if the game gets serious.
  - Transition cost: builder's judgment, with one constraint — the game must keep visibly evolving while any engine work happens. Chose both lanes: steady visual gains now, background thread toward a higher ceiling.
  - Audience: personal today; would consider going public if it became exceptional. Do not foreclose release.
  - Engine identity: not pride-driven. His actual question is whether one stack covers every vertical he imagines. All four cutting-edge flavors named as genuinely exciting: visuals, animation/feel, living-world simulation, AI-driven content.

- 2026-07-31, wish-list walkthrough (owner chat, same day — a session-shaped monologue of the game in his head; captured faithfully, organized by system):
  - Session open ritual: sign on → check the crew (who leveled, who is away on tasks, where they are on the map) → check territory (did enemies come through) → read the map for what changed → pick the day's mode. Wants a different thing to do every day: caves one day, portals another, founding a new territory, or a crew-development day (gear, stances, promotions).
  - Crew: roster capacity grows with his own character's level. Members have personalities and initiate contact — badger him for new weapons, get bored, want action and growth. Not upkeep; social pressure from the roster.
  - Embodied dispatch: sent companions are real entities in world space, not menu timers. Send the assassin ahead to scout terrain and report back; a cavalier carries a message to a distant member; a party sent to a cave can send one runner back asking for backup because they are outclassed. No easy recall — sent means gone until they physically return.
  - Real-time squad tactics with Fire Emblem DNA: formation commands (cluster on him, spread, front line / back line — Wild Rift teamfight structure); adjacency buffs when two members buddy up mid-fight (the FE support-pair idea in real time); team combo abilities with their own animations, gated on fielding the right comp; recon as an ability (his or a member's) revealing enemy team compositions at distance. Enemy clusters have their own comps; bosses have their own teams. Third-party bands can stumble into a fight in progress — two-on-one decisions. Cadence: "RuneScape meets Wild Rift" — ability resets leaving just enough time to steer strategy.
  - Contextual combat modifiers, each a visible single cause: cloud cover over you raises stealth; shooting from farther adds arrow velocity; falling adds force to a sword swing. Strength holds weight: a berserker carries himself differently than a thief or a swordsman — per-class movement styles and animations, class-consistent stats, wanted early.
  - Map: fills in as explored, but re-grays on login proportional to time away — the world changed, anomalies obscured areas, new map appeared. Exploration state decays because the world moves.
  - Anomalies/portals (Solo Leveling reference): severity scales a terrain-transformation radius — low barely changes scenery; high transforms a 100 m / 1,000 m / 2,000 m zone that carries its difficulty inside. Open anomalies are beacons that draw nearby NPCs. Red/hard variants lock you in until the boss dies (or a ~10-second escape window at the threshold). Normal anomalies stay open — NPC teams can wander in mid-clear, which he finds funny.
  - Resources (Albion reference): high-grade nodes on long respawn timers; special nodes spawn randomly; NPCs in proximity fight over good nodes the way Albion clans did.
  - Territory: not a concrete settlement — destructible, weathers, heals over time.
  - Safe zones: multiple settled areas for trade, barter, smithing, banking. Outside them the world runs; NPCs wander out and get wrecked.
  - Death/recovery (extends the settled no-permadeath doctrine): downed members are revived/recovered; truly lost ones go to a retrievable place (graveyard-like) and retrieval is its own excursion. A healer class can revive mid-fight but only under uninterrupted cover — revival is a process, never instant. Items/spells as alternate revival paths. The risk should make the rewards worth it.
  - Bosses: RuneScape PvM model, explicitly no story bossing. Randomly generated bosses with unknown attributes and lurking teams; difficulty variance is the novelty engine. Badass items and NPC-built settlements scattered in the world.
  - Multiplayer: distant-future openness to real players making it "massively ridiculous." Keep the door open.
  - Look target — ANSWERS the deferred look question: Ghost of Tsushima named as the graphics he loves, blended with Fire Emblem, Wild Rift, and Elder Scrolls. Armor, weapons, and character visuals should look amazing. Wind, light, and color palette painterliness — not gritty photorealism.
  - Auto-ability triggers (added same chat): classed members carry configurable pre-fight trigger policies — autopilot scenarios set before the fight, per member, per situation. A healer defaults heal-heavy in some fights, support-and-fight hybrid in others. Modifiers "obviously more extensive than that." Lineage: FF12 gambits / Dragon Age tactics, composed with the real-time squad layer. Note: this is also what dispatched parties run unsupervised — the runner-asking-for-backup behavior falls out of trigger policies executing away from him.
  - North-star framing (his words): the game should be built as if "premium going into the future" is the direction of travel.
  - Look target refined by rejection (same chat, after reviewing references): Kena — "nice but too Disney," lacks the GoT seriousness; Ghost of a Tale, Valheim, Tchia — all rejected for his game. The binding read: the target is GROUNDED and SERIOUS, not painterly-cute — GoT's gravity, muted palette, weather with menace, a world that reads mature. He imagines a future "dude player base," possibly competitive open-world. Cute, cozy, whimsical, and storybook registers are all wrong for this game at any fidelity level.
  - THE BENCHMARK, settled after a second review round: **Enshrouded — "pretty close wow, very close if it had all the other elements i liked, this is up there."** The closest named visual target: serious stylized-realism, volumetric fog and light, ruins, muted palette. Elden Ring endorsed twice over — "good mechanics to have and i like the graphics there." His own synthesis of the whole target: "imagine GoT and if Wild Rift was third person, with the seriousness energy, Elder Scrolls exploration with everything we mentioned."
  - Combat spec by rejection: Dark and Darker — likes the free manual attack style as an element, but "purely manual whack-a-mole" cannot carry the team/ability/animation mechanics he wants; rejected as a model. Naraka — "too quick, fight speed isn't weighted"; rejected. Bannerlord — not close. The spec that survives: third-person ABILITY-KIT combat (Wild Rift class kits and cooldowns) at a WEIGHTED, deliberate pace (Elden Ring), with room for manual strikes inside it — never twitch-speed, never pure manual flailing, always able to express team mechanics, combos, and class animations.
